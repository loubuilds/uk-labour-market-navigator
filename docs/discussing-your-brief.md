# Talk it through, or get the brief

Once the official evidence is checked, your assistant offers a choice:

> Would you like to talk through what this means for your role and offer, or shall I draft the brief now? A short discussion can help tailor the next steps to your offer, recruitment messaging and candidate experience.

A quick brief gives you a starting point. A short discussion helps turn that evidence into more relevant questions and next steps: the statistics and AI cannot supply your knowledge of the role, offer or candidate experience. This is a research companion, and your contribution matters.

Discussion is optional: if you already asked for a report straight away, the assistant proceeds. You can return to the conversation later. Neither route replaces professional review, and the report does not claim that a human discussion or review has occurred merely because it was generated.

## What a discussion adds

The statistics describe the wider market. Your professional context helps identify which practical questions matter next. For a pay proposal, that might mean connecting current pay evidence with candidate feedback and documenting improvements to how the full employment offer is communicated. These checks can strengthen the business case without making a fair-pay review conditional on recruitment changes.

Depending on your question, useful discussion topics include:

- Where candidates stop progressing, and what feedback is available.
- Whether the advert clearly communicates pay, hours, on-site expectations, flexibility, benefits, development and working environment that are actually offered.
- Whether the careers site and recruitment marketing reinforce those same reasons to join.
- Which comparable local adverts and public employer information could help assess how the offer is presented alongside alternatives.

The assistant asks about the relevant next topic, rather than working through a compulsory checklist. Broad, non-confidential context is enough; the report does not need internal recruitment records.

## Keep findings and possible explanations distinct

These are suggested investigations, not conclusions that an employer's pay or messaging caused a hiring problem. Public employer reviews, ratings and social content may reveal themes worth checking, but are selective signals and do not prove what attracts candidates or makes hiring successful.

The official figures keep their source definitions and dates. Discussion can shape the interpretation and suggested actions; it cannot establish missing candidate availability, create local salaries, or turn company information into checked official evidence. Reviewing external adverts and employer information is not an automated capability of this release.
